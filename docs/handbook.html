1The system at a glance

Belin-Pok runs a menswear shop that sells caps, tees, jackets, sweatshirts, shorts, joggers, boxers and socks — over the counter to walk-in customers, in bulk to businesses buying wholesale, and online. One system keeps all three in step.

1.1 What Belin-Pok does

  • Stock: every size and colour of every product is tracked separately. Deliveries are logged in seconds, and stock comes off automatically when anything sells, in store or online.
  • Point of sale: a till for the counter, with retail, wholesale and factory-pack prices, split payments (cash, mobile money, card, on account), and shortcuts for busy moments.
  • Online shop: a full website (and a phone app) where customers browse, order for pickup or delivery, and track orders. Online prices carry a configurable markup over in-store prices.
  • Wholesale accounts: approved business customers can buy on credit up to a limit, with a complete ledger of charges and payments.
  • Owner oversight: sales reports for any day, week or period; a receipt-by-receipt sales journal; and an activity log of everything staff did — built for an owner who isn't always in the shop.

1.4 Principles behind the design

Nothing changes without a record

Stock never changes by editing a number: every change is a movement (received, sold, corrected, returned) with who made it and why, so the history always adds up to what's on the shelf. Customer credit works the same way. Mistakes are fixed with offsetting entries (a void, a correction), not by erasing history. Only an owner can delete outright, and even that leaves a note in the activity log.

The server decides prices and permissions

Screens show prices and hide buttons for convenience, but the API re-prices every sale and order itself and checks every request against the person's role. A tampered request can't sell below price, oversell stock, or do something a role doesn't allow.

Built for a real counter

Busy hours, staff without barcode scanners, phones and tablets at the counter, mobile money and buying on account — the till has shortcuts for all of them (quick log by type, one-amount baskets broken down later, split payments) without losing accuracy.

2Running the shop

A practical guide to the admin system at admin.belpok.xyz, organised by task. What each person sees depends on their role; anything they can't do is simply not shown.

2.1 Signing in

  • Sign in with an email address, a phone number (for staff invited by phone — type it any way, e.g. 024 123 4567 or +233 24 123 4567), or a username (the first owner account).
  • Sessions stay signed in on that device and renew themselves; changing your password signs you out everywhere else.
  • After 10 wrong passwords in 15 minutes, that account is paused for 15 minutes.
  • Forgot your password? Staff with an email can request a reset link (valid for 1 hour). Anyone else asks an owner for a new sign-in link from Staff.

2.3 Products and stock

Receiving a delivery

Receive stock (on Products, Inventory or a product's page) is the fast path. It stays open after each item so a whole delivery can be logged in one go.

  • New item: take a photo, type a name and how many came in. It's saved as a draft priced at ₵0 — not on sale anywhere yet. Category, price, type, size and colour can be filled in now or later. For broad items, leave the name blank and choose a category: the item is named after it ("Joggers").
  • Already in the catalogue: search for it, pick the type/size/colour (or add a new one), enter the quantity.

A product's life

StatusMeaning
DraftBeing set up. Not on the website, app or till. Fast-received items start here.
LivePublished: on the website, the app and the till. Publishing needs a price.
ArchivedDiscontinued: off the website and app. Leftover stock can still be sold at the till.

Types, sizes, photos and stock levels

  • Each type/size/colour combination is its own SKU (generated automatically) with its own stock, prices and low-stock alert level (default 5).
  • Types hold kinds or qualities of one broad product at different prices — e.g. Joggers in Standard (₵130), Premium (₵150) and Branded (₵180), or Unbranded / a brand name. Add a type on the product page creates a type across several sizes at one price. Shoppers choose a type first on the website and see its price; the till shows each option's price.
  • Photos: upload several at once; choose the cover photo shoppers see first. JPEG, PNG or WebP.
  • Descriptions: Write a description from the photo asks Claude (Anthropic's AI) to draft one from the cover photo and the product's details, plus the main colour it sees. It's only a draft in the box — check it, correct anything wrong (a photo can't show fabric or whether a brand is genuine) and save. About 1–2 US cents per draft; needs the Anthropic key in 5.1.
  • Inventory shows stock for every SKU, what's running low (the reorder list), and the full movement history.
  • Adjust records a restock, a count correction, a customer return, or a sale made outside the till — always with a reason.
  • Repack records loose units organised into factory-style packs (a note; the count doesn't change).

2.4 Prices: in-store, wholesale, pack and online

PriceWhere it's setWho pays it
In-storeThe product's price, or a size's own priceWalk-in customers at the till
WholesalePer size (blank = in-store price)Wholesale customers at the till
PackPer size: a price for a whole factory pack, and pieces per packWholesale customers buying sealed packs
OnlineAutomatic: in-store price + the shop's online markup (Shop settings), rounded up to a tidy amount — unless a size has its own fixed online priceCustomers on the website and app

Set the online markup

The markup starts at 0%, so online and in-store prices are the same until an owner sets it under Shop settings. The page shows a worked example table before saving. Changes reach checkout at once and the website's pages within a minute. Orders already placed keep their price.

Wholesale and pack prices are never shown to the public: the website and app only ever receive online prices.

2.5 Selling at the till

The Point of sale page has the product grid (search and category chips), the current sale, and today's receipts below.

  1. Choose Retail or Wholesale. Wholesale asks for the customer first and unlocks wholesale and pack prices.
  2. Tap products to add them: pick the size (sold-out sizes are struck through), the price tier, and the quantity. The till won't add more than is in stock.
  3. Press Charge. Enter how the customer paid — split across several methods if needed (e.g. part cash, part mobile money). On account appears only for approved wholesale customers and only up to their available credit.
  4. Complete sale. The API re-prices every line, checks stock and takes it off, and records any credit charge — all at once, or not at all.

2.6 Busy-hour sales

Two shortcuts keep the queue moving when finding exact products would slow things down. Both are tidied up later from To sort out under Today's sales; until then their stock hasn't come off, and the dashboard shows a reminder.

Quick log by type

Record a line by type of item ("Caps"), price and quantity. Later, Identify attaches the exact product (stock comes off then), or Split it if it was really several different products.

One amount

Take the money for a whole mixed basket as a single amount, with an optional note ("2 tees, a cap and socks"). Later, Break down lists what was in it — exact products, or just types — and only saves once the parts add up to exactly what was taken.

2.7 Voids, returns and corrections

A customer returns goodsAdjust stock → Customer return on that size.
Stock count doesn't matchAdjust stock → Correction with the difference and a reason.
A customer's balance is wrongOn their page: Correction (up or down, with a reason).
Test data, or something that should never have existedOwner only: Delete (see 2.12).

2.8 Online orders

  • Orders from the website and app land as Pending. No payment is taken online: the shop calls the customer to confirm and arrange payment, pickup or delivery.
  • Move each order along — Paid → Packed → Shipped → Delivered — with one click, adding a note (e.g. a mobile money reference) if useful. Every change is recorded in the order's timeline and the activity log.
  • Cancelling puts the order's items back in stock, and so does refunding an order that hadn't been shipped yet. A refund after shipping leaves stock alone — record a customer return when the goods actually come back. Reopening a cancelled order takes the stock again, if it's still there.
  • Each line shows the online price charged and what it would have cost in store; the order shows the markup it earned.
  • A warning panel lists orders needing more stock than the system shows — usually a counter sale that wasn't rung up. Check the shelf before packing.
  • Customers using the phone app get a notification when their order moves on (once push notifications are configured — see 5.1).

2.9 Customers and credit

  • Customers are created when someone signs up on the website or app, when a guest places an order, or by staff (Add customer) — typically wholesale businesses.
  • A wholesale account must be approved before it can buy on credit. Set its credit limit (0 = cash only).
  • Each wholesale customer has a credit account: owes / limit / available, a usage bar (red past 85%), and every charge, payment and correction with who recorded it.
  • Record payment when they pay something off; the dashboard lists who owes the most and for how long (red past 30 days).

2.11 Managing staff owner

  1. Staff → Invite staff. Enter their name, an email and/or a phone number, and tick their roles.
  2. Choose how to send the invite: Email (sent automatically when email is set up, otherwise your email app opens), WhatsApp, SMS, your phone's Share menu, or Copy. The message is written for you, including the link and what to sign in with.
  3. They open the link (valid once, for 7 days), choose a password, and they're in. Someone invited by phone only signs in with that number.
  • Change roles with the pencil next to someone's roles. Switch someone off with the Active toggle: they can't sign in, but their history stays. Owners can't change their own roles or switch themselves off.
  • Sign-in link issues a fresh link — for a forgotten password or an expired invite — with the same sending options.

2.12 Deleting test data owner

For records that should never have existed — a sale rung up while trying the till, a test order — an owner can delete outright. A reason is required; the activity log keeps a red Deleted entry saying what it was, who deleted it and why.

RecordWhereWhat happens
Till saleSales journal (open the receipt) or Today's salesGone from the journal and reports; stock back; credit charge off the customer's balance.
Online orderThe order's pageGone from orders and reports; its items back in stock.
CustomerThe customer's pageOnly once they have no orders, sales or credit history. Addresses and website login go too.
Stock entryInventory → Movement historyOnly entries made by hand; their effect on stock is undone.
ProductThe product's pageIncluding its stock history — but not while any sale or order includes it.

Deletion is refused, with an explanation, where undoing isn't possible: credit already paid off, received stock since sold, or a product still in a sale.

2.13 Editing the website

Online shop → Website (owners and inventory managers) changes what shoppers see on belpok.xyz without touching code. Edit, then Save changes; the site shows it within a minute, and the activity log records who changed which section.

  • Announcement strip — the thin bar above the header (turn it off, or use it for a sale).
  • Home page banner — small heading, headline, text, two buttons (each to any shop page or web address), and up to three photos (otherwise the newest product photos).
  • Selling points — up to four, each with an icon.
  • Home page sections — show or hide "Shop by category" and "New arrivals"; a hand-picked Featured row of up to 12 live products, in your order.
  • Wholesale section, footer text, contact details (also on the wholesale page) and social links.

2.14 A daily routine

Opening & during the day

  • Check the dashboard: low stock, orders needing attention.
  • Log deliveries with Receive stock as they arrive.
  • Move online orders along as they're paid and packed.
  • Use quick log / one amount when busy.

Closing

  • Sort out today's quick-logged and one-amount sales.
  • Sales reports → Today: count the drawer against Cash.
  • Review voids and their reasons.
  • Owner: skim the activity log.

3.5 Sign-in and security

  • Passwords are hashed with argon2. Access tokens last 15 minutes; refresh tokens last 30 days, are single-use and rotate on every refresh — reuse of an old one signs that person out everywhere.
  • Invite links last 7 days, reset links 1 hour; both work once. Sign-in pauses after 10 failures in 15 minutes.
  • Every staff request checks the person is still active, so switching someone off takes effect immediately.
  • The public catalogue endpoints return only live products and online prices — never wholesale, pack or in-store prices.
  • Secrets live only in Railway's service variables, never in the repository.

3.6 The web storefront (apps/web)

Next.js 16 with React 19 and Tailwind CSS 4, running as a Node server. Catalogue pages (home, shop, categories, products) are rendered on the server and cached for a minute, so they're fast, indexed by search engines, and show rich previews when shared. Cart, checkout and accounts run in the browser; the cart is kept on the shopper's device.

Pages: home, shop (search, filters, sorting — all in the URL), category, product, cart, checkout (guest or signed in; pickup or delivery), order confirmation, account (orders and addresses), sign-in, register, password reset, wholesale, sitemap and robots.

3.7 The admin system (apps/admin-web)

React 19 + Vite, React Router, TanStack Query (data caching and refresh), Recharts, Tailwind CSS 4. Built to static files served by Caddy. Pages live in src/pages/, shared components in src/components/, and the API client, session and helpers in src/lib/. Data refreshes when the window regains focus, so a tablet left at the counter catches up with sales made elsewhere.

3.8 The phone app (apps/storefront)

Flutter, with shared models, API client and session in packages/belpok_core. Browsing, cart, checkout and order history against the same API, plus push notifications. Android and iOS projects are configured (app ID com.belpok.storefront); publishing needs your Play Console and Apple Developer accounts — see the README's release section.

4.2 How a change goes live

  1. Commit and push to main.
  2. Railway rebuilds only the services whose files changed (watch paths): API, storefront and admin are independent.
  3. For the API, the pre-deploy step runs python -m app.migrate first. A failed migration stops the deploy, and the previous version keeps running.
  4. The new version takes over once its health check passes (/health for the API).

Check progress with railway deployment list --service <name> or in the Railway dashboard. To roll back, redeploy an earlier deployment from the dashboard — but note a migration that already ran is not undone by a rollback.

4.6 Backups and recovery

All business data lives in the Railway Postgres service; product photos are in the bucket.

Railway backups need a Pro workspace

Railway's scheduled database backups (and its one-click restore) are only available on its Pro plan, which this workspace isn't on — so there are no backups on Railway yet. Upgrading, then turning on daily, weekly and monthly backups for the postgres-volume, is the most reliable protection.

Your own backups, in the meantime

scripts/backup-db.sh        # saves ~/belpok-backups/belpok-YYYYMMDD-HHMM.sql.gz

Run it from the repository on a computer signed in to Railway — weekly, and before any risky change. It dumps the whole database inside Railway, downloads it, and refuses to keep a dump that stopped part-way. The files hold customer details, so keep them private; copy them somewhere off the computer (an encrypted drive or private cloud folder) too.

To restore a backup into a database (a fresh local one to inspect, or a new Railway database):

createdb belpok_restore
gunzip -c ~/belpok-backups/belpok-YYYYMMDD-HHMM.sql.gz | psql -d belpok_restore

The first backups were taken (and one test-restored) on 23–24 October 2026. Product photos aren't in it; the bucket is separate.

5.2 Known limitations

  • No online payment. Orders are confirmed and paid by arrangement with the customer.
  • A one-amount basket counts as 1 item in "items sold" until it's broken down.
  • Issuing a new sign-in link doesn't cancel older unused links; each still works once until it expires (or until any of them is used).
  • Wholesale customers buy in person; there's no self-serve wholesale ordering online.
  • Cost prices aren't recorded, so the system reports revenue, not profit.
